01THE PRINCIPLE

A room that
keeps unfolding.

Two mirrors can make a corridor seem endless. Give the corridor a curved geometry, and the reflections begin to tell a different story.

A dodecahedron lined with one-way mirrors and traced in light already suggests an infinity chamber. The trouble is ordinary space: its flat geometry leaves gaps at the corners. In hyperbolic space, the dodecahedra can meet perfectly, each face opening onto another.

A / FLAT · CUBETILES PERFECTLY

Eight cubes fill 4π steradians at a corner. The mirror corridor closes.

B / FLAT · DODECAHEDRONGAPS REMAIN

Angles refuse to close. The reflections break at the vertices.

C / HYPERBOLIC · DODECAHEDRONSPACE FILLS

Curvature makes room for a repeating tessellation.

03THE INSTRUMENT

Build it,
in light.

A physical simulation of the object itself: index 1.30 at the core rising to 1.85 at the corners, one-way film on every face, LED strips on every edge. Rays bend through the gradient, refract at the surface, and keep reflecting, losing a little light to the plastic on every pass, until the glow pools on the plinth below.

04THE OPEN QUESTIONS

Beautiful idea.
Hard physics.

01

Can the materials blend?

The plastics would need a wide range of refractive indices while staying clear and mixing smoothly.

TRY ↗ 02

Will the gradient look smooth?

Printer layers might leave visible steps in the ray paths; finer layers or post-print diffusion could help.

TRY ↗ 03

What happens to color?

Different wavelengths bend by different amounts. Narrow-spectrum LEDs could keep the geometry legible.

TRY ↗ 04

Can the corners close?

The outside medium and the shape of the faces affect whether the intended angles meet at each vertex.
